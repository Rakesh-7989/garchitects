## Choosing the Right Kitchen Layout

The layout of a kitchen is dictated first by the shape of the available space, and second by how you actually cook. The four most common layouts are:

- **Straight (Single Wall):** Best for compact spaces and open-plan living. Works well when cooking is infrequent.
- **L-Shaped:** The most versatile layout. Allows a natural work triangle between the hob, sink, and refrigerator.
- **U-Shaped:** Maximises storage and countertop area. Ideal for large kitchens and homes where cooking is central to daily life.
- **Island Kitchen:** A status statement — but only practical when the kitchen has sufficient floor area (minimum 12 feet wide) and the family lifestyle supports it.

## Materials: Choosing Wisely

Indian kitchens deal with heat, steam, oil, and intense use. Material choices must balance aesthetics with durability:

- **Shutters:** HDHMR (High-Density Moisture-Resistant) board is the preferred substrate. PU (Polyurethane) paint finishes offer superior durability over regular laminates.
- **Countertops:** Granite remains the most practical choice — it handles heat, stains, and heavy use without complaint. Quartz offers a more refined appearance but is sensitive to heat. Avoid marble unless you are willing to maintain it.
- **Hardware:** Invest in quality soft-close hinges and drawer channels. Inferior hardware is the single most common reason a kitchen feels cheap — and deteriorates within five years.

## Storage Planning: The Overlooked Priority

The most common regret in a new kitchen is insufficient storage. Before finalising the design, audit everything you currently store in your kitchen and plan a specific place for each category. Tall pull-out units for dry groceries, deep drawers for pots and pans, and a dedicated waste management unit are non-negotiable in a well-planned kitchen.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Insufficient lighting:** Task lighting under wall cabinets is essential, not optional.
2. **Inadequate ventilation:** An exhaust fan placed too far from the hob is ineffective. A chimney should be positioned directly above the cooking zone.
3. **Ignoring the work triangle:** Hob, sink, and refrigerator should form a compact, unobstructed triangle.
4. **Over-designing for aesthetics:** Glass shutters and open shelving look beautiful in photographs — but require constant maintenance in active Indian kitchens.
