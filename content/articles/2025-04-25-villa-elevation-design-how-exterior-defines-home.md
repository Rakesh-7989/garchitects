## Elevation is Not Decoration — It is Architecture

A common misconception is that the elevation (or facade) is the last thing to design — a decorative layer applied after the floor plan is fixed. In reality, the best elevations emerge from the inside out. The position of rooms, the hierarchy of spaces, and the flow of the plan should be expressed on the exterior — not concealed by applied ornamentation.

## The Role of Proportions

Proportion is the most powerful tool in elevation design. The relationship between solid wall and void (window/door), between horizontal and vertical elements, and between the ground floor and upper floors determines whether a home looks balanced and grounded — or top-heavy and awkward.

At G Architects, we work through multiple proportion studies before arriving at an elevation. This process, invisible to the final observer, is the difference between a facade that looks effortlessly right and one that simply looks finished.

## Material Selection for the Hyderabad Climate

Hyderabad's climate — hot, dry summers and moderate monsoons — places specific demands on exterior materials:

- **Exposed brick and stone:** Durable, low-maintenance, and develop character with age. The warm tones of locally available Kota stone or Deccani stone harmonise naturally with the landscape.
- **Plaster finishes:** Textured plaster is more forgiving than smooth finishes, which show every crack and stain.
- **Stone cladding:** Used selectively, natural stone cladding adds depth and permanence. Avoid applying it across the entire facade — selective use creates far more visual impact.

## Lighting the Elevation

Night-time lighting transforms an elevation. Well-placed exterior lights — uplighting at the base of stone cladding, strip lights along horizontal banding, and spot lighting at the entrance — create a facade that reads as premium and welcoming after dark. This is rarely planned at the design stage and frequently retrofitted poorly as an afterthought.

## The Entry Experience

The entry sequence — gate, driveway, porch, and front door — is the most important part of the elevation experience. A home that unfolds gradually, from street edge to threshold, creates a sense of arrival that no amount of ornamental detailing on the facade itself can substitute for.
