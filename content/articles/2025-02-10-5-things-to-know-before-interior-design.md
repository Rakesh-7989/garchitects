## 1. Define Your Style Before You Meet the Designer

Before the first meeting, spend time understanding what you like. Browse references — whether it's a warm, wood-toned contemporary look or a sleek, minimal aesthetic with neutral tones. Having 10–15 images that resonate with you will save weeks of back-and-forth and ensure the designer creates something that truly feels like yours.

## 2. Understand What the Budget Includes

A common source of confusion is what a quoted budget covers. Always clarify whether the cost includes civil work (flooring, false ceiling, walls), furniture, fixtures, lighting, and accessories — or only some of these. At G Architects, we provide itemised breakdowns so clients know exactly where every rupee is allocated.

## 3. Functionality Comes Before Aesthetics

A beautifully designed kitchen that lacks adequate storage or counter space will frustrate you daily. Always start with the functional brief — how many people cook? Do you need a study nook? Is the master wardrobe for one or two? Great design solves problems first and looks beautiful second.

## 4. Material Selection Affects Both Cost and Maintenance

The difference between MDF with PU coating, HDHMR, and solid wood is not just price — it is longevity and maintenance. Similarly, glossy laminates show fingerprints while matte finishes age more gracefully. Understanding these trade-offs before committing to materials will save money and disappointment in the long run.

## 5. Plan for Lighting from Day One

Lighting is often treated as an afterthought, decided after walls are plastered and wiring is done. This is a mistake. Lighting — both natural and artificial — defines how a space feels. Layer it: ambient, task, and accent. The right light can make a compact room feel expansive; the wrong light can make a spacious room feel dim and cold.
